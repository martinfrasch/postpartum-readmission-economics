When does the model pay?

A risk model only earns money when it beats the two strategies that need no model: contact every new mother, or contact none. Move the cost of one outreach contact and watch where that happens.

How a strategy is scored. Contacting one patient who would have been readmitted is worth effectiveness × readmission cost. The model contacts everyone whose recalibrated risk exceeds contact cost ÷ that value, the rational cut-off. The value of every strategy is computed from the same test-set patients.

Why the window sits where it does. When contacting is cheap, contacting everyone wins and the model can only lose readmissions it fails to flag. When it is expensive, few patients have enough risk to justify it. A model helps most near the cost where contacting everyone and no one break even, which is prevalence × value per readmission reached.

Data. To respect the data-use agreement, risks are grouped into four bins per model, each with at least 11 readmissions and 11 non-readmissions. The model can therefore only contact whole bins, so its curve is coarser, and its gains generally smaller, than the patient-level figures in the manuscript (results/revision/economic_check.json).
