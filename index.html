When does the model pay?

A risk model only earns money when it beats the two strategies that need no model: contact every new mother, or contact none. Move the cost of one outreach contact and watch where that happens.

How a strategy is scored. Contacting one patient who would have been readmitted is worth effectiveness × readmission cost. The model contacts everyone whose recalibrated risk exceeds contact cost ÷ that value, the rational cut-off. The value of every strategy is computed from the same test-set patients.

Why the window sits where it does. When contacting is cheap, contacting everyone wins and the model can only lose readmissions it fails to flag. When it is expensive, few patients have enough risk to justify it. A model helps most near the cost where contacting everyone and no one break even, which is prevalence × value per readmission reached.

States (post hoc, exploratory). nuMoM2b recruited at eight US centres and has no state variable. For each state, test-set participants are reweighted (raking) so that their race and ethnicity, Medicaid coverage, education, BMI and age match that state's first births in 2022–24 (CDC WONDER). This assumes that readmission risk given these characteristics is the same everywhere; it captures differences in who gives birth, not in how care is delivered. To keep every published count at 11 or more, only state-level summaries are published: the curves for a state scale the test-set readmission rates to that state's reweighted rate and keep the model's ranking as in the test set, so they show how the economics shift with the state's readmission rate and prices, not with its AUC (shown separately). Intervals for a state are approximate: resampling is scaled to its effective sample size. West Virginia is omitted (fewer than 11 effective readmissions). Medicaid prices are amounts paid per claim line in 2022–23 (T-MSIS), for a home visit (CPT 99347–99350) and a physician telephone call (99441–99443); states with fewer than 1,000 claim lines use the national mean.

Data. To respect the data-use agreement, risks are grouped into four bins per model, each with at least 11 readmissions and 11 non-readmissions. The model contacts a whole bin when the bin's average predicted risk reaches the cut-off, so its curve is a coarse step function and differs from the patient-level figures in the manuscript (results/revision/economic_check.json).
